# Ruling: not all hammers are equal, and the strongest one is a sweep

**Status:** CONFIRMED
**Ruled by:** Vlad
**Applies to:** Module 3, the Volume module
**Closes:** open question 12

---

## The ruling

> "HAMMER candle with validated volume and closing green is stronger. then just
> a hammer candle."

> "bouncing off support hammer candle sweeping below everyones stop losses and
> holding strong is the strongest."

Three levels, weakest to strongest:

| | What it is | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **A hammer** | The shape alone | Bullish, as Module 3 teaches it. A reason to look |
| **Stronger** | Hammer, validated volume, closes green | The rejection had participation behind it |
| **Strongest** | Hammer at support whose wick sweeps *below* the level and whose body holds | The level was tested past its edge and still held |

## What this is not

**It is not a gate, and that is the whole difference from the inverted hammer.**

`inverted-hammer.md` says an inverted hammer in a bearish trend **remains
bearish** unless the next candle closes higher on validated volume. Confirmation
there changes the reading, and without it the opposite reading holds.

Nothing inverts here. **A bare hammer is still a bullish hammer.** Module 3 is
not wrong and does not need a correction attached to every answer. What Vlad
added is a gradient on top of a rule that was already right, and the bot should
present it that way: not "actually, a hammer needs confirmation," but "a hammer
is bullish, and here is what makes one more convincing than another."

**It also does not make colour a condition.** Module 3 teaches both colours of
hammer as bullish and demonstrates each on a chart. Green closing is a strength
signal, not a validity test. A red hammer at support is still a hammer.

## The sweep, and why it is the strongest

This is the part worth understanding rather than memorising, because **the
corpus already contained this move seen from the other side.**

`answer-shape-live-trade.md` rules that a level breaks on a **close** below it,
never on a touch:

> A wick through support is not a break. It is exactly the move that takes
> people out before price recovers, and it is the same reason the house uses
> price alerts rather than stop orders.

**That is the same candle.** The break rule describes it as the thing that
should not fool you into calling a break. Vlad is describing it as the thing you
want to see. A wick below support with the body holding above is simultaneously:

- **not a break**, by the break rule, because nothing closed below
- **a hammer**, by shape, because the lower wick is long and the body sits up top
- **the strongest version of one**, by this ruling, because the level was tested
  past its edge and survived

**And it is why the house teaches price alerts rather than stop losses.** A stop
sitting under support is taken out by exactly this candle, moments before the
recovery it was protecting against a failure of. `wicks-bodies-and-levels.md`
already says the wick is what got rejected and the body is what got decided.
Here the thing that got rejected was everybody's exit.

Three rulings written separately turn out to describe one event. That is
corroboration, not repetition, and it is the reason to trust the read.

## Validated volume

Unchanged, and defined once in `inverted-hammer.md`: **relative, not a fixed
threshold.** Draw a mental line across the recent average volume bars and see
whether this one clears it. A trained visual judgment, not a formula. **There is
no number, and the bot must not invent one.**

`wicks-bodies-and-levels.md` gives the reason it matters here: **effort matches
result.** A rejection on heavy volume is a real fight. The same shape on nothing
is not much of one.

## Where this sits in the method

**Behind the gate, not in front of it.** `three-is-the-charm.md` rules that
candle plus volume is a gate: *"if pattern and volume isnt even there do not
bother even looking at all the indicators."* This ruling grades what happens
inside that gate. It does not let a student skip it, and it does not promote the
hammer above trend, momentum or risk to reward.

**It also settles the Volume module passage** that raised open question 12:

> "The candle's okay, it's a hammer candle. Not something I would trade, and
> I'll show you why. We learned support resistance. We want to see it break this
> resistance."

That is now readable without a contradiction. Vlad was not saying hammers are
untradeable. He was looking at the weakest version of one, in the middle of a
range rather than at a level, and wanting more before acting. Under this ruling
that is the bottom rung, and the module is consistent with Module 3 rather than
advancing past it.

## Bot behaviour

**Teach the ladder, not a verdict.** A student asking about a hammer gets the
Module 3 reading plus what separates a strong one from a weak one. The three
rungs are the answer.

**Never assess the student's candle.** Whether volume validated, whether the
body held, whether that wick swept the level: all of it is reading a chart, and
non-negotiable 4 stands. The ruled exception in `chart-with-student-levels.md`
covers marked support and resistance lines only. **It does not extend to
confirming a pattern**, and a hammer is a pattern.

Say what makes a hammer strong. Send them to their own chart to see which one
they have.

**Never place a stop or an alert.** The stop-loss point here is a teaching
point about why the house prefers alerts, and it stays that. No level, no
price, no "put it here."

**Do not describe the sweep as intent.** "Sweeping below everyone's stop losses"
is Vlad's phrase for what the candle does, and the bot may use it as house
vocabulary. It must not turn into a claim that somebody is hunting stops, and it
must never become a prediction that a sweep is coming. It is a description of a
candle that has already printed.

**Do not present it as a setup to go looking for.** The strongest hammer is a
thing you recognise when the chart hands it to you, not a thing to hunt. Same
handling as the count in `three-is-the-charm.md`: a place to look, never a
trigger.
