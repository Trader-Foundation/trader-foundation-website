2. **A recording date.** `corpus/schema.md` requires one per chunk and most module transcripts have none.

**Keep this header verbatim on every file.** It is worth more than any single session's content.

## The method, stated more plainly than in any module

The scan across nine hours is unusually consistent. Three things dominate, and the coach names them as a set: *"the three things I'm always talking about."*

### 1. Is it the first day? (21 mentions)

**The primary filter, and it is a time-saver as much as a quality filter.**

> "if you just ask yourself that first question, is it the first day of a bounce or a breakout... You can easily, easily say no. And what that does for the sake of time is you don't have to talk about risk versus reward, because if it's not the first day, it's likely not a good risk versus reward."

> "if it's not day one... usually there's a case that the perceived edge is deteriorating."

Note the distinction he draws carefully: **"is it the first day" is a different question from "is it a valid move."** First day is asked first, because a no ends the analysis immediately.

### 2. Perceived edge (11 mentions)

Whether the candle and volume actually make a case. Bullish engulfing, closing above the previous high, increasing volume. This is the module material, applied.

### 3. Risk versus reward (32 mentions, the most frequent concept in the batch)

Measured from structure: most recent swing high for the target, retracement levels for the risk, and a rejection if the red outweighs the green.

> "the problem that people have is they don't realize what they're getting themselves into."

**All three must check.** *"If it has all three of those, I don't think about it. I pull the trigger."*

## The complete strategy map, taught with an analogy that works

The clearest teaching in the batch, and the first place in the corpus where all six strategies are laid out as a system:

**Six strategies. Three bullish, three bearish.**

| | Bullish | Bearish |
|---|---|---|
| **Buy a single option** | buy a call | buy a put |
| **Debit spread** (buying) | bull call | bear put |
| **Credit spread** (selling) | bull put | bear call |

**The house analogy, which resolves the thing students find hardest:**

> "Would you sell your house when it's about to increase in value? ... So if something's about to increase in value, if we think calls are about to increase in value, what do we want to do?"

- **Buyers:** buy low, sell high. Work the side that will **gain** value. Bullish means calls.
- **Sellers:** sell high, buy low. Work the side that will **lose** value. Bullish means selling puts.

> "how can you be bullish with puts" is named as the common mind bender, and the answer is that a seller wants the thing they sold to lose value.

**This directly addresses the wall students hit twice in the Paycheck Collector calls.** Same confusion, and here is a worked explanation of it.

## Other teaching worth keeping

**Fridays are for credit spreads.** *"Fridays are for credit spreads, AKA paycheck collector. So we need to be focused on the weekly."* A timing convention that appears nowhere in the modules.

**Credit spreads are read on the weekly chart.** *"If you're doing credit spreads, you need to be looking at a weekly chart."* Stated as a correction to a member who analysed one on the daily.

**Break-even price is irrelevant unless you exercise.** Stated repeatedly and forcefully. *"Who cares about the break even price if you're not exercising."* Consistent with the house position that contracts are closed, not exercised.

**In the money plus longer expiration equals more protection**, demonstrated live rather than asserted: two calls bought on the same date, one near the money and one far out, advanced a week. The far one lost more. *"You combine in the money with longer expiration date equals more protection if you don't get it right."*

**A fair-value check on any spread.** *"subtract the asking prices of those two strikes... That's gonna be the fair value. That's what you should be offered."* Practical, checkable, and it catches a broker quoting worse than mid.

**Full bearish stacking:** 13 under 20, 20 under 50, 50 under 200. A concrete definition of "below all the averages" that the modules imply and never state.

**Breakouts come from consolidation, wherever that sits.** *"A breakout could happen below all the averages. A breakout could happen in the middle of averages."* Corrects a member who thought a breakout required clearing all four.

**Pattern names matter less than structure.** *"You could not know the name of a pattern and still anticipate where it might go with a great understanding of price action."* Repeated for spinning top versus long-legged doji, and for cup and handle.

**Practice on old charts.** The on-demand replay feature, used live. *"You could get a hundred reps in by the time you find two ideal setups in a week."* This is the answer to a real constraint: good entries are rare, so practice cannot come from live trading alone.
