## The structural finding: these carry timestamps

**The single biggest blocker on this project is solved for this source class.**

`corpus/schema.md` requires a timestamp on every chunk and will not index without one. No plain-text transcript supplied so far has carried one, which is why twenty sources are processed and nothing is indexed.

This session arrived as an **SRT caption file** with **368 timestamped cues across 22 minutes**. Every sentence has a position. A chunk from it can cite `live session, 2026-08-06, 00:02:14` and a student can go straight to that moment.

**The implication reaches past this file.** The classroom modules exist as videos. The transcripts supplied were plain text, but the same videos would produce captions with timestamps through the same route that produced this one. **If the modules can be re-exported as SRT or VTT, the timestamp blocker disappears for the whole corpus**, and the only remaining blocker is module numbering.

That is worth more than anything else in this session, and this session is genuinely good.

## What this source class is

Not a module and not a one-to-one coaching call. A recurring market review:

- Revisits setups called in previous sessions and grades how they played out
- Reads the market top down, SPY on the weekly
- Screens live, page by page, rejecting most candidates out loud
- Teaches from whatever comes up

**The grading of past calls is unique to this class.** No module shows a setup that failed. This one shows two setups that reached the same target where one continued and one rejected, and draws the lesson from the pair.

## Teaching worth keeping, none of it in any module

### "Sometimes" is the enemy. Make it conditional

> "sometimes you do stuff. It needs to be conditional. Like when I have this, this is what I do. When I have that, that's what I do. You don't just want to vague sometimes."

> "If it's just a gut feeling, I'm telling you, it's just gonna backfire down the road."

This is the discipline principle the modules gesture at and never state. It is also the operational form of **trade the chart, not your heart**: a conditional rule is chart-driven by construction, and "sometimes" is where the heart gets in.

### The answer to "should I have held longer?"

The best passage in the session, and it answers a question students must ask constantly.

Two trades. One reached the planned exit and kept going. One reached it and rejected.

> "The thing that is the same is it met that zone... Whether it went beyond it or got to it and rejected, what remains the same between both is it got there. And during that moment, that's what we know for sure. It reached our plan, now we must execute our plan."

**The outcome after the exit is not evidence about the exit.** Both trades were identical at the moment of decision, and only one of them looks like a mistake afterwards. That is a complete answer and the bot should have it.

### "The hiccup", a named disqualifier for the bounce setup

> "It does have that initial bounce where it goes lower, then pushes up, but it's just a very slight fall. It doesn't truly sell off into this bounce. It's up, up, up, up, hiccup, boop, right back. I want a true fall. Give me a few bearish candles before I see buyers step in again."

**A refinement of the core bounce setup that no module contains.** The bounce needs a real decline to bounce from. A shallow dip inside an uptrend looks like the pattern and is not it.

The screening criterion is stated plainly as **"goes lower, then pushes up"**, with the bearish mirror **"goes higher, then pushes down."**

### Looking for functionality, not colours

> "I'm not on here just looking for red and green candles. Looking for functionality."

Said while rejecting most of five screener pages out loud. The rejections teach more than the acceptances, which is a thing only this source class shows.

### The charts do not care

> "they're not a real thing. It's not a living thing. It has no feelings... it doesn't care about what you want."

Vlad's ruling from a different angle, and a useful one to have in the corpus in someone else's words.

### Anchor the Fibonacci to the move being traded

> "naturally you want to take your Fibonacci from all the way up here... But really, this fib should be started from right here."

Drawn from the actual swing rather than the whole visible chart. A refinement of Module 8 and a common student error.

### Structure sets the target, and the target may not justify the risk

Mapping a lower low, then asking where a lower high could form and whether there is room:

> "there could be room up to there, but your risk may be greater than that... So a little more red than green... Doesn't seem very favorable."

Risk and reward assessed from structure before the trade is considered, and the trade rejected on that basis.

## The news case study

The session's centrepiece. A member sent a message about a company being awarded a contract, with a strong recommendation. The coach checks the daily, the weekly and the monthly, finds nothing on any timeframe, and shows the stock falling for the months after.

> "There's no way in hell this is based off a chart."

> "if you rely on the news, it's because you don't fully trust what you're doing here on the charts, and that is just a result or byproduct of not practicing enough."

**This is the fullest statement of the house position on news anywhere in the corpus**, and it connects straight to Module 2, which says direction is unknowable without inside information.

**It also matters for a compliance item already logged.** The Moving Averages module contains "you got to have some sort of insider information," which was excluded because stripped of context it reads as advice to obtain it. This session is the corrective in the curriculum's own voice, and it is a better answer to "how do I trade the news" than the exclusion left behind.

**Redaction:** the member is not named, but their message is quoted verbatim. Marked as a member message with the sender redacted. The substance is kept because the teaching does not work without it.

## Compliance

**Clean.** No outcome claims, no percentage returns, no win rates, no earnings language, no guarantee words.

Consistent with the pattern: this session has no motivating passage. It reviews, screens, rejects, and teaches. Nothing in it is selling.

## Open questions

1. **Who is speaking?** The register differs from the module recordings. If this is a coach rather than Vlad, then where a session contradicts a ruling the ruling wins, and that needs to be knowable per file.
2. **"Beyond the Bollinger Bands. Might even be at the third."** A third band is not a standard two-deviation setup. Either a non-standard configuration or a reference to something else. Do not encode.
3. **Are the modules available as caption files?** The highest-value question in the project right now.
